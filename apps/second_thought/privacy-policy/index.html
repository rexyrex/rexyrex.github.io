Privacy Policy for Second Thought

Effective date: 2026-11-16

Second Thought (“the app”) is a free screen-time app by Rexyrex. When you open an app or website you picked, it shows a short pause so you can decide whether you really want in. The app has no ads, no account and no subscription.

This policy explains what the app handles, what stays on your device, and the small amount of anonymous technical data that is sent to help fix problems.

1. What stays on your device

Everything you set up and everything the app records about your habits is stored only on your device, using the operating system’s standard app storage. This includes:

  • the apps and websites you picked, your pause, unlock, limit, schedule and focus settings, and your to-dos;

  • your statistics (attempts, skips, time saved, streaks, per-app numbers);

  • your intentions, mood answers and journal entries.

None of this is sent to us or to anyone else. It is removed when you uninstall the app (statistics can also be reset in Settings). “Export data” copies it to your clipboard only when you ask.

2. Device features the app uses

Accessibility Service (Android). With your permission, Second Thought uses Android’s Accessibility Service API to notice which app is in front and, in supported browsers, the address of the page, so it can pause the apps and websites you picked. It does not read messages, passwords or other screen content. What it sees is used on the device in real time and is never collected, stored off your device, or shared.

Usage access (Android, optional). If you grant it, the app reads Android’s screen-time statistics to show them in Insights. These numbers stay on your device.

Screen Time (iOS). On iPhone the app uses Apple’s Screen Time frameworks (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity). The apps you choose are handled by iOS as private tokens; Second Thought cannot see their names or your browsing, and nothing from Screen Time leaves your device.

Camera. The optional Mirror exercise shows the front camera for a moment before an app opens. Nothing is recorded, saved or sent.

Motion sensors. The “turn your phone” exercise reads the device’s motion sensors while it is on screen. The readings are not stored or sent.

Notifications (iOS). The app may post a local notification to start a pause. Notifications are created on the device.

3. Anonymous data sent to help improve the app

The app uses two Google services:

  • Google Firebase Analytics: anonymous usage events (for example app opens, session length and which screens are used), app version, device model, operating system, language and a random app-instance identifier.

  • Google Firebase Crashlytics: crash and error reports, including the stack trace, device model, operating system, app version and the state of the app at the time of the failure.

This data is not linked to your name or any account, is not used for advertising, and is not used to track you across other companies’ apps or websites. It never includes the apps or websites you picked, your statistics, your journal or anything the Accessibility Service or Screen Time sees. It is sent over an encrypted connection (HTTPS/TLS).

Google processes this data for us as a service provider. See Firebase privacy and security and Google’s privacy policy.

4. How we use the data

  • to find and fix crashes and bugs;

  • to understand, in aggregate, which features are used so we can improve the app.

We do not sell or share data with anyone else, and the app contains no advertising.

5. Retention and deletion

Data on your device is kept until you delete it or uninstall the app. Anonymous analytics and crash data are kept by Google for a limited period under the project’s retention settings and then deleted. Because this data is not tied to your identity, we usually cannot single out your records. If you want us to try, email us and we will help where we can.
