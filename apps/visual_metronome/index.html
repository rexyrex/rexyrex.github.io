Nineteen ways to see the beat

Every visualizer runs on the same clock as the sound, so what you see lands when you hear the click. Each one also shows the next beat coming, which lets your eye lead the sound instead of chasing it.

  • Pulse Orb A sphere that draws breath before each beat and pops on it.
  • Timeline Upcoming clicks slide toward a playhead, like a tape.
  • Bar Map One pip per beat, a cursor sweeping the bar, a big count.
  • Focus Ring One thin ring fills across the beat and snaps on it.
  • Classic A wooden wind-up metronome with a sliding tempo weight.
  • Pendulum A bob swings to an extreme on every beat.
  • Bounce A ball hops from pad to pad, landing on every beat.
  • Newton's Cradle End balls swing out and click back in on every beat.
  • Orbit A comet circles once per bar, flaring at every beat mark.
  • Rings A seed swells before each beat and bursts into a ring.
  • Meter Bars One bar per step; each shoots up on its click and decays.
  • Heart Monitor An ECG trace sweeps the bar; each beat is a spike.
  • Strobe A hard full-screen flash on every beat, brackets closing in between.
  • Vinyl A record turns once per bar; beat marks pass under the stylus.
  • Light Grid A perspective floor rolls toward you, one line per beat.
  • Ripples A drop falls through each beat and lands on the click.
  • Burst Sparks gather to the center, then explode on the beat.
  • Thunder A charge builds in the cloud; the bolt strikes on the beat.
  • Kaleido A petal wheel that turns one notch per beat, like an escapement.

Set the tempo your way

Anywhere from 30 to 300 BPM, with the classical marking underneath, from Grave to Prestissimo.

  • Drag the tempo ruler, or press minus and plus. Hold them to keep going.
  • Tap the number to type an exact tempo.
  • Tap along on the Tap button and the app averages your last few taps.

Count any bar

Play in 2/4, 3/4, 4/4 or 6/8, in quarters, eighths, triplets or sixteenths. Subdivisions tick quietly between the main beats.

Tap a beat to make it accented or silent. Mark the downbeat, or drop the beats you want to feel on your own.

Program a groove

Switch on a pattern and the single click becomes a step sequencer. It starts from kick, snare and hi-hat, and you can run up to six tracks, each with its own sound, in quarter, eighth or sixteenth steps.

Practice with a plan

The tempo trainer moves the tempo toward a target while you play, by a step you choose every few bars.

The gap trainer plays a few bars, then silences a few. The visuals keep going, so you can check whether you are still on the beat when the click comes back. Both change at the next bar line.

Keep your setups

Save the tempo, meter, subdivision, accents and sound as a preset, and pick up where you left off next session.

Fill the screen

Tap the visualizer to open it fullscreen, with the tempo in the corner. The display stays awake while the metronome plays, so the phone can sit on a music stand and keep time.

  • Audio delay compensation, from −100 to +300 ms, lines the click up with what you see when you play through Bluetooth headphones or a slow speaker.

No account, no ads

Your tempo, sounds, patterns and presets are saved on your device. There is no sign-in and no cloud sync. The app uses Firebase Analytics and Crashlytics to see how it is used and to fix crashes, and it never records audio or asks for your location. Read the privacy policy
